01 / About me

Curious about the why behind the numbers.

I’m a Computer Science and Engineering graduate from Lovely Professional University, now building my career in data and business analytics. I enjoy taking an open-ended business question, finding the right data, and turning the analysis into a clear next step.

My projects span retail business intelligence, customer churn, sales forecasting, and predictive modelling. I work across the analysis lifecycle: cleaning and validating data with Python and SQL, exploring patterns, then communicating useful findings through Power BI, Tableau, and visualisations.

My approach is practical and evidence-led: understand the decision first, check the quality of the data, make the reasoning easy to follow, and keep recommendations grounded in what the analysis can actually support. I’m currently based in Riyadh on a Transferable Iqama and looking for an entry level analyst role where I can learn from a team and contribute from day one.

Retail business intelligence · January 2026

Making a large retail dataset useful for decisions

Business problem
Understand sales performance and customer behaviour across a large retail dataset.
Method
Cleaned, analysed, and validated 400K+ rows with Python and SQL, then built Power BI views with forecasting and churn analysis.
Finding
The analysis surfaced £8.83M in revenue alongside patterns in customer behaviour and sales trends.
Recommendation
Use the dashboard to investigate churn and sales patterns by customer and period before prioritising retention or growth actions.

Finding

Retail sales exploration · February 2025

Exploring what moves retail sales

Business problem
Explore how Walmart sales trends compare with external factors such as temperature, holidays, and CPI.
Method
Used Python, Pandas, Seaborn, and Matplotlib for exploratory analysis and created an interactive dashboard.
Finding
The analysis was designed to identify potential risks, growth opportunities, and revenue drivers; no numerical effects are claimed in the CV.

Use the exploratory patterns to guide deeper investigation, and test candidate drivers against additional periods and relevant business context.
